# 0022: the words may lean, but only as far as they stay readable

*Hermes, orchestrating the owner's words-in-the-world brief, 29 September 2026. Status: proposed (owner to accept).*

The veranda's words-on-glass layer says of itself, in its own header: it is "a 2D overlay in both tiers, so on the live tier it does not lean with the camera the way the picture does (a render target would)" (`packages/scenes/veranda/stage-element.js:19-21`). That was the right call when the words only needed to pass behind pillars and settle clear of them. The owner's brief now asks for the opposite: text blocks whose supporting plane is driven by quaternions, soft-constrained toward readable orientations.

This decision records the departure and the bounds on it, because the two rules that make words-on-glass trustworthy are both threatened by a turn.

**Rule one is that the words are real text.** They are: Pretext flows them line by line through a shape function and never measures the DOM (`packages/type/layout.js:80-105`), and the layer's only output is one absolutely placed span per line inside the block it came from (`packages/type/index.js:46-63`). A turn must not disturb that. So the orientation is a **composited transform on a layer the words sit in**, never a change to the geometry they were laid out with. A turn cannot reflow a line, cannot break a word, and cannot take the text out of the accessibility tree.

**Rule two is that occluded words come forward.** The pane's settle enforces it per line, at most 15% hidden (`packages/scenes/veranda/pane.core.js:211-215`, `settleSpot` at `:230-289`), and the occlusion itself is an SVG mask the element builds from the depth grid and assigns in stage space (`stage-element.js:573-588`), deliberately frozen while the camera moves (`:567-572`). Two consequences follow. The transform may not go on the element that carries the mask, or the mask would travel with it and the occlusion would be wrong; it goes on a layer inside the panel. And the settle, which measures real line boxes, would see foreshortened rects through a turn, so the applier must be able to hand a measured rect back through its own transform: `unproject(rect)`, the flat pre-transform rect. Coverage is then judged in the plane's own space, which is where the 15% rule was written.

**The bounds.** A plane may turn only inside a readability cone (`READABLE.limitDeg`, 35 degrees from the viewer's axis), and it is projected back onto that cone rather than clamped per axis, so a corner never leaves the cone while another leaves it further. The register changes the model, not the colour: quiet turns nothing (maximum tilt 0), warm allows 6 degrees, playful 16, and reduced motion is identity in every register. Motion is a damped shortest-path slerp whose rate does not depend on frame rate, with a deadband so it comes to rest without jitter. Idle sway is seeded, so the same page at the same moment is the same drawing.

**Where the maths lives.** `packages/core/quat.core.js` (unit quaternions, slerp, damped settle, a matrix for CSS) and `packages/core/orient.core.js` (the cone, the register parameter surface, the seeded sway), both pure and tested in Node, budgeted apart from the element under decision 0010. `packages/core/orient.js` is the browser half. The repo had no orientation maths at all before this: the live camera is pitch only (`camera.rotation.set(s.cam.pitch, 0, 0)`, `packages/stage3d/depth-photo/depth-photo.gl.js:300`), and `cameraAt` (`packages/stage3d/stage3d.core.js:97`) carries a single pitch angle.

**Accepted cost.** Leaning text in the live tier is not the picture leaning: the words are composited over the drawing, so at a large tilt a page's words can sit on a plane that does not match the drawing's own perspective. That is why the cone is narrow in warm, why playful tops out at 16 degrees, and why the veranda wiring is opt-in (a `pane-turn` attribute, off by default), so every existing check and every existing page behaves exactly as before.

**Follows from this:** the slerp camera and focus paths (the next primitive) reuse `quat.core.js` and the same settle discipline; a scene that wants text on a genuinely oriented surface in 3D still wants a render target, which stays a separate, later question.
